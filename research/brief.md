# Silvering Homes: sector brief for trainers

Silvering Homes is a **fictional** housing association. It is modelled on the kind of mid-sized, community-based landlord found across England: a former council stock transfer, now running its own repairs service and building a modest number of new homes each year. Every person, scheme, supplier and figure in this pack is invented. Numbers in documents come from the pack's own database, so learners can check Copilot's answers against the source.

## How a housing association works
- **What it is.** A not-for-profit registered provider of social housing, here a Community Benefit Society. Surpluses are reinvested in homes and services. It is governed by a board of non-executives with committees for audit, finance, customer experience and governance.
- **Tenures:**
  - social rent (formula rent, roughly 50-60% of market);
  - affordable rent (up to 80% of market);
  - shared ownership (buy a share, rent the rest);
  - older persons' and supported housing (sheltered and extra care);
  - market rent;
  - leaseholders (mostly former right-to-buy flats in blocks).
- **Income.** Mostly rent and service charges. Rents rise each April under the government rent standard (CPI + 1% from 2026). Rising arrears matter because many tenants are moving onto Universal Credit, where the housing element is paid to the tenant unless an Alternative Payment Arrangement is in place.
- **Spending.** Mostly on repairs, planned works (kitchens, bathrooms, roofs, windows), building safety, staff, and interest on the debt that funds new homes. Grant from Homes England helps fund new homes.

## Regulation the scenarios draw on
- **Regulator of Social Housing (RSH).**
  - Since April 2024, the RSH has inspected large landlords against the **consumer standards**: Safety & Quality, Transparency, Influence & Accountability, Neighbourhood & Community, and Tenancy.
  - It grades **C1-C4** for consumer standards, and **G1-G4 / V1-V4** for governance and viability.
  - Silvering's fictional C2 means "some weaknesses; improvement needed".
- **Tenant Satisfaction Measures (TSMs).** Each year, 22 measures are reported to the RSH and published to tenants:
  - 12 come from a perception survey (TP01-TP12: overall satisfaction, repairs, safe home, listening, complaints, ASB and so on);
  - 10 come from management data (complaints per 1,000 homes, repairs in target, gas/fire/asbestos/water/lift compliance, Decent Homes, ASB cases).
  - Results are split into **LCRA** (rented) and **LCHO** (shared ownership).
- **Awaab's Law** (from 27 October 2025). Legal timescales for hazards in social homes:
  - emergency hazards made safe within 24 hours;
  - significant damp and mould investigated within 10 working days;
  - a written summary to the resident within 3 working days of the investigation;
  - safety works started within 5 working days.

  Landlords must offer alternative accommodation if they can't make a home safe.
- **Housing Ombudsman Complaint Handling Code** (statutory from April 2024):
  - stage 1: acknowledge within 5 working days, respond within 10;
  - stage 2: acknowledge within 5 working days, respond within 20.

  An annual self-assessment is required. Residents can escalate to the Ombudsman, which can find maladministration and order compensation.
- **Building safety.**
  - The **big six**: gas (annual LGSR), electrical (EICR at least every 5 years), fire risk assessments, water hygiene (legionella), asbestos and lifts (LOLER, 6-monthly).
  - **Higher-risk buildings** (18m+ or 7+ storeys) are registered with the Building Safety Regulator and need a safety case and a resident engagement strategy. At Silvering, that means Rosemount Tower.
- **Decent Homes Standard.** Homes must be free of serious hazards (assessed under HHSRS), in reasonable repair, with reasonably modern facilities and reasonable warmth.

## Where Silvering's problems are (the planted story)
- **Brenchley Gardens** (1960s estate). Damp and mould, slow repairs, repeat complaints, the lowest satisfaction and two non-decent homes.
- **Routine repairs.** Performance fell over summer 2026, because of trades vacancies and more use of a subcontractor.
- **Awaab's Law.** Some cases missed a legal timescale: late investigations, late written summaries, late works starts.
- **Complaints.** Stage 1 response times slipped from June to September 2026. Satisfaction with complaint handling is low.
- **Rosemount Tower.** Fire door actions are overdue. Another block's fire risk assessment and a lift inspection are overdue.
- **Arrears.** Rising slowly, concentrated in Universal Credit households.
- **Orchard Fields** (new build). Many defects, and homes waiting for first lets.
- **The AI pilot.** Its main risk is oversharing: vulnerability and salary data are stored where too many people can see them.

## Typical Copilot uses in housing
- Answering "is this an emergency?" questions from the repairs policy.
- Drafting empathetic stage 1 complaint responses from a case history.
- Theming hundreds of TSM free-text comments.
- Summarising board and committee papers.
- Writing Awaab's Law written summaries in plain English.
- Triaging shared mailboxes with safeguarding escalation.
- Analysing repairs, voids and arrears in Excel.
- Building a contact-centre agent grounded on the policies.
- Preparing case chronologies for ASB or Ombudsman cases.
